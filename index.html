Australian Census history · 1911–2021

A Nation, Counted

Every five years, almost every household in Australia fills in the same form. Read side by side, 110 years of Census results show how a small, young, mostly British and largely rural country became a big, older, urban and multicultural one.

What is the Census? The Census of Population and Housing counts every person in Australia on one night and asks the same questions of each household: age, birthplace, religion, language, work and home. The Commonwealth statistician, today's Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), has run it since 1911, and every five years since 1961.

Because the questions barely change, the Census is the best record we have of how Australian society has changed. This page follows five threads through the data: where we live, how old we are, where we were born, what we believe and who gets counted.

Chapter 1

Where we live

Australia is one of the world's emptiest continents, yet one of its most urban nations. Each Census has found more of us living in the capital cities and along the coast.

From 38% to 67%

When the first national Census was taken in 1911, the capital cities held 38% of all Australians. Most people still lived on farms or in country towns.

2 in 3 Australians lived in one of the eight capital cities in 2021 (67%).

Sydney has been the largest city at every Census, but Melbourne has never been far behind: 41,000 people behind in 1911 and 286,000 behind in 2021. Newer centres such as the Gold Coast and the Sunshine Coast appear on the map only from the 1960s, as Australians moved north for the sun.

1916–17: during World War I most states shrank as more than 300,000 men sailed overseas.

Around 1950: post-war migrants and the baby boom pushed growth to 3% a year or more in every state.

2021: with international borders closed during COVID-19, Victoria shrank for the first time since 1917 and NSW for the first time since Federation.

Growth hugs the city edges

Between the 2011 and 2021 Censuses, the fastest-growing council areas were new suburbs on the fringes of the big cities.

+110% Camden, on Sydney's south-western edge, more than doubled from 56,737 to 119,325 residents.

Wyndham (+81%) and Melton (+64%) on Melbourne's western edge, and Serpentine-Jarrahdale (+81%) south of Perth, were close behind. Many inland and remote areas, especially in Western Australia's mining regions, lost residents.

A coastal, urban people

The dots crowd into a thin crescent from Brisbane to Adelaide and a pocket around Perth. Inland, whole regions the size of European countries hold only a handful of dots.

90.3% of Australians lived in urban areas in 2021, up from 57.5% in 1911.

Chapter 2

Who we are: a nation growing older

The 1911 Census found a young country: two in five people were aged under 20. In 2021, there were more people in their 50s than in their teens.

Two forces behind an ageing nation

Longer lives. A girl born around 1921 could expect to live to 63; a girl born around 2021 can expect to reach 85.

Smaller families. Births per woman fell to 2.2 in the Great Depression, soared to 3.55 in 1961 at the height of the baby boom, then dropped below the "replacement level" of 2.1 in 1976. They have stayed below it ever since.

1.7 babies per woman in 2021, the lowest of any Census year.

Replacement level is the number of babies per woman needed for each generation to replace itself without migration: about 2.1.

Chapter 3

Where we come from

Every Census has asked where people were born. The answers show the end of a mostly British Australia and the arrival of the world.

A low point in 1947

At Federation almost one in four people had been born overseas, most of them in the British Isles. Immigration almost stopped during the Depression and World War II, and by 1947 the overseas-born share had fallen to 9.8%, the lowest ever recorded.

Then came the post-war migration program, followed by the end of the White Australia policy in the 1970s. By 2021 the share was 29.3%, the highest of any year shown here.

1921: four out of five overseas-born residents came from the United Kingdom or Ireland.

2021: India (712,040) and China (584,660) were the second- and third-largest birthplaces, ahead of New Zealand.

Nepal had 130,290 residents in 2021, more than the United States, Germany or Greece.

Chapter 4

What we believe

The religion question has been on every Census since 1911, and it has always been optional. In 1911 only 0.2% of people said they had no religion. In 2021 almost two in five did.

Christianity is still the largest group, but its share has halved in 50 years, from 86.2% in 1971 to 43.9% in 2021. Catholics (20.0%) now outnumber Anglicans (9.8%) two to one, a reversal of 1911, when the Church of England was the largest denomination.

Migration has brought new faiths: Islam (3.2%), Hinduism (2.7%) and Buddhism (2.4%) together make up more than 8% of Australians. The biggest change, though, is the rise of people reporting no religion, especially among younger Australians.

Chapter 5

Counting everyone

For its first 60 years, the Census did not count everyone. Until 1967, section 127 of the Constitution said that "aboriginal natives shall not be counted" in the official population.

A referendum that changed the count

On 27 May 1967, 90.77% of voters approved removing section 127. It remains the largest "Yes" vote in any Australian referendum.

812,728 people identified as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander in 2021, 3.2% of everyone counted and seven times the 1971 count.

The count grew by 25% between 2016 and 2021 alone. Less than half of that rise (43.5%) came from births, deaths and migration; the ABS attributes the rest to more people identifying as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander and to better Census coverage.

The next chapter

Notes on the data

Census counts and estimates
Older series use Census counts. Since 1971 the ABS also publishes the estimated resident population, which adjusts the count for people missed on Census night. Each chart states which one it uses.
Data preparation
Original ABS spreadsheets and CSV files were filtered, reshaped and summed into the small files in /data using the scripts in /scripts. No values were estimated or changed.
